Male seahorses carry the eggs and give birth to the young.

Hippos produce a reddish oily fluid that works like a natural sunscreen.
Squirrels help plant forests, because many of the nuts they bury are never dug up and sprout into trees.

Some parrots can live for more than 80 years.

# Ocean and kelp
Giant kelp can grow up to about 60 centimeters (2 feet) in a single day.
Kelp isn't a plant. It's a type of brown algae.
Sea otters wrap themselves in kelp so they don't drift away while they sleep.
Sea otters protect kelp forests by eating sea urchins, which would otherwise mow the kelp down.
Giant kelp can grow to more than 45 meters (150 feet) long.
Kelp stays upright using little gas-filled floats called pneumatocysts.
Alginate from kelp is used to thicken foods like ice cream and to make toothpaste smooth.
Scientists estimate that roughly half of the oxygen we breathe comes from the ocean, mostly from tiny phytoplankton.
The Pacific Ocean is bigger than all of Earth's land put together.
The deepest known point in the ocean, the Challenger Deep, is nearly 11 kilometers (7 miles) down.
If Mount Everest were dropped into the Mariana Trench, its peak would still be about 2 kilometers underwater.
More than 80% of the ocean floor has never been mapped in high detail.
The ocean holds about 97% of all the water on Earth.
Sound travels about four times faster in water than in air.
The Great Barrier Reef is the largest structure built by living things, and it can be seen from orbit.
Coral is an animal, not a plant or a rock.
Most creatures in the deep sea can make their own light.
Anglerfish lure prey toward their mouths with a glowing growth on their heads.
The longest mountain range on Earth is underwater: the mid-ocean ridge runs about 65,000 kilometers.
Sea cucumbers can push out their own internal organs to scare off predators, then grow them back.
Clownfish are all born male, and the dominant one in a group can become female.
Sea urchins have no eyes, but they can sense light with their tube feet.
Some whale calls can travel hundreds of kilometers underwater.
Sharks have existed for longer than trees.
Greenland sharks can live for at least 250 years, making them among the longest-lived vertebrates.
An octopus can squeeze through any gap bigger than its hard beak.
The ocean sunfish is one of the heaviest bony fish and can weigh over 2,000 kilograms.
Most of the salt in the ocean came from rocks on land, washed into the sea by rivers.
If all the salt in the ocean were spread over the land, it would form a layer about 150 meters thick.
Hydrothermal vents on the sea floor support whole ecosystems that live without sunlight.
The ocean looks blue because water absorbs red light and scatters blue light.
Sea turtles often return to the same beach where they hatched to lay their own eggs.
Antarctic krill may have one of the largest total masses of any wild animal species on Earth.
Seahorses are fish, even though they swim upright.
A pistol shrimp snaps its claw so fast that it makes a bubble that briefly gets nearly as hot as the Sun's surface.
Sea otters have the thickest fur of any animal, with up to about a million hairs per square inch.
Walruses use their tusks to haul themselves out of the water onto ice.
Kelp forests are found along cold, nutrient-rich coasts on every continent except Antarctica.

Jupiter has the shortest day of any planet, just under 10 hours.

Olympus Mons on Mars is about two and a half times as tall as Mount Everest.
Dust storms on Mars can grow large enough to cover the whole planet.
Sunsets on Mars look blue.
A year on Mercury lasts just 88 Earth days.
Uranus spins on its side.
Neptune has the fastest winds in the solar system, over 2,000 kilometers per hour.
By most estimates, there are more stars in the universe than grains of sand on all of Earth's beaches.
Space is silent, because sound needs air or another material to travel through.
The International Space Station circles Earth about every 90 minutes, so astronauts see around 16 sunrises a day.
Astronauts can grow a few centimeters taller in space because their spines stretch out without gravity.
The Moon is drifting away from Earth by about 3.8 centimeters every year.
The same side of the Moon always faces Earth.
Pluto is smaller than Earth's Moon.
A day on Pluto lasts about six and a half Earth days.
Halley's Comet comes back about every 76 years. Its next visit is in 2061.
Some of the starlight you see tonight left its star thousands of years ago.
The Sun holds about 99.8% of all the mass in the solar system.
Earth is the only planet in the solar system that isn't named after a god.
Saturn's rings are mostly ice, and in places they're only about 10 meters thick.
Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a storm bigger than Earth that has lasted for centuries.
Venus is the hottest planet, even hotter than Mercury, because its thick atmosphere traps heat.
The first animals to go to space and come back alive were fruit flies, in 1947.
Laika the dog became the first animal to orbit Earth, in 1957.
Yuri Gagarin became the first person in space in 1961.
Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed on the Moon on 20 July 1969.
Twelve people have walked on the Moon.
Voyager 1, launched in 1977, is the farthest human-made object from Earth.
The Voyager probes each carry a golden record with sounds and pictures from Earth.
A black hole's gravity is so strong that not even light can escape it.
Astronomers have found planets that orbit two stars, like Tatooine in Star Wars.
Earth's spin is very slowly slowing down, so days get a tiny bit longer over millions of years.
Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, during winter in the Northern Hemisphere.
Seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis, not by how far Earth is from the Sun.
The Moon has quakes, called moonquakes.
On Mercury, temperatures swing from about 430°C in the day to about -180°C at night.
Light from the Moon takes a little over one second to reach Earth.
Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos.
Jupiter has more than 90 known moons.
A spacecraft has touched the Sun's outer atmosphere: NASA's Parker Solar Probe, in 2021.

# Human body
An adult human skeleton has 206 bones.
Babies are born with around 300 bones, and many of them fuse together as they grow.
The smallest bone in your body, the stapes, is in your ear.
The femur, your thigh bone, is the longest and strongest bone in your body.
The cells lining your stomach are replaced every few days.
Humans share about 98 to 99% of their DNA with chimpanzees.
Your heart beats around 100,000 times a day.
Fingernails grow about two to three times faster than toenails.
You can't tickle yourself because your brain predicts the feeling before it happens.
Your skin is your body's largest organ.
An adult has about 5 liters of blood.
A red blood cell lives for about 120 days.
Your brain uses about 20% of your body's energy, even though it's about 2% of your weight.
Your brain is nearly 60% fat.
You shed tens of thousands of skin cells every minute.
Your taste buds are replaced about every two weeks.
The "funny bone" isn't a bone. It's the ulnar nerve running past your elbow.
Goosebumps are a leftover reflex that once made our ancestors' body hair stand up.
Humans glow very faintly, but the light is about 1,000 times too weak for our eyes to see.
Your body has roughly as many bacterial cells as human cells.
Hiccups are caused by sudden spasms of the diaphragm.
Babies are born with kneecaps made of cartilage, which slowly turn into bone.
Your left lung is smaller than your right one to make room for your heart.
The hyoid bone in your throat is the only bone that isn't connected to another bone.
Your heart has its own electrical system and can keep beating for a short time outside the body.
Cracking your knuckles makes noise from gas bubbles in the joint, and studies haven't linked it to arthritis.
Your body contains about 3 to 4 grams of iron, enough to make a small nail.
The average person has around 100,000 hairs on their head.
Tooth enamel is the hardest substance in the human body.
Even identical twins have different fingerprints.
Your nose and ears keep growing slowly throughout your life.
The human eye blinks around 15 to 20 times a minute.
Your body makes roughly a liter of saliva every day.
Your bones are constantly being rebuilt, and you get a mostly new skeleton about every 10 years.
Humans are one of the very few animals that cry emotional tears.
The acid in your stomach is strong enough that your stomach needs a layer of mucus to protect itself.
Each eyelash lasts a few months before it falls out and is replaced.
Your sense of smell is closely linked to memory, which is why a scent can suddenly bring back a moment.
Newborn babies can't see colors well; their color vision develops over the first months.
