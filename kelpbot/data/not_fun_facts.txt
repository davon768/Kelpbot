# Sad
The last known Tasmanian tiger died in a zoo in Hobart in 1936.
Martha, the last passenger pigeon, died at the Cincinnati Zoo in 1914. Her species had once numbered in the billions.
Lonesome George, the last known Pinta Island tortoise, died in 2012.
Toughie, the last known Rabbs' fringe-limbed tree frog, died in 2016.
Sudan, the last male northern white rhino, died in 2018. Only two females of his kind are left.
The baiji, a river dolphin that lived in China's Yangtze River, was declared functionally extinct in 2006.
The dodo was gone less than a century after people first wrote about it.
The fourth global coral bleaching event, which began in 2023, has affected more than 80% of the world's reef area.
Hachikō the dog kept waiting at a Tokyo train station for nearly ten years after his owner died.
Laika, the first animal to orbit Earth, was never meant to come home and died within hours of launch.
The Voyager probes will eventually run out of power and go silent, but they'll keep drifting through space for billions of years.
The Mars rover Opportunity went silent in 2018 when a dust storm blocked the sunlight it needed to recharge.
By common estimates, only about 1 in 1,000 sea turtle hatchlings survives to adulthood.
Some adult mayflies live for less than a day.
Mozart was buried in a shared grave, and nobody knows exactly where his body lies.
Vincent van Gogh sold very few paintings in his lifetime. Now they sell for tens of millions.
In 2018, an orca named Tahlequah carried her dead calf through the ocean for 17 days.
Glacier National Park had around 150 glaciers in 1850. Only about 25 are still big enough to count.
In 2019, Iceland held a funeral for Okjökull, the first of its glaciers lost to climate change.
Nikola Tesla died in debt, alone in a New York hotel room.
Edgar Allan Poe died at 40, and the cause of his death is still a mystery.
Australia's 2019–20 bushfires killed or displaced an estimated 3 billion animals.
The Aral Sea, once the fourth-largest lake in the world, has mostly dried up.
More than 99% of all species that have ever lived on Earth are now extinct.
Only about 3% of Earth's water is fresh, and most of that is frozen.
Amelia Earhart disappeared over the Pacific in 1937 and has never been found.
None of the musicians in the Titanic's band survived the sinking.
The Titanic had lifeboat seats for only about half of the people on board.
Many large dog breeds only live around 8 to 10 years.
A worker honeybee in summer usually lives for only about six weeks.
Male honeybees die right after they mate.
Arctic sea ice at its late-summer low has shrunk by roughly a third or more since satellite records began in 1979.
In about 5 billion years, the Sun will swell into a red giant and Earth will likely be scorched or swallowed.
In roughly a billion years, the Sun will be bright enough to boil away Earth's oceans.
As the Moon slowly drifts away, total solar eclipses will stop happening in roughly 600 million years.
Saturn's rings are slowly raining down onto the planet and could be gone in a few hundred million years.
Most people can't remember anything from before they were about three years old, and those memories won't come back.
Without review, people tend to forget most new information within a few days.
Researchers have linked long-term loneliness to health risks comparable to smoking.
Around 40% of the world's roughly 7,000 languages are endangered.
The playwright Aeschylus wrote somewhere between 70 and 90 plays. Only 7 survive.
About three-quarters of American silent films are lost forever.
Dozens of early Doctor Who episodes are missing because the BBC erased or threw out the tapes.
NASA's original high-quality recordings of the Apollo 11 moonwalk were probably erased and reused.
Your phone's battery loses a little capacity every time it goes through a charge cycle.
The great auk was hunted to extinction; the last confirmed pair was killed in 1844.
Steller's sea cow was hunted to extinction just 27 years after Europeans first described it.
The golden toad of Costa Rica was last seen in 1989.
The Pyrenean ibex went extinct in 2000. A clone born in 2003 lived for only a few minutes.
The western black rhino was declared extinct in 2011.
The quagga, a half-striped relative of the zebra, went extinct in 1883.
Some cicadas spend 17 years underground and then live as adults for only a few weeks.
Elephants have been seen returning to the bones of their dead and touching them gently with their trunks.
Hummingbirds burn energy so fast that most nights they drop into a near-hibernation state to avoid starving.
Pacific salmon die shortly after they spawn.
Female octopuses usually stop eating while guarding their eggs and die soon after they hatch.
Up to a billion birds are estimated to die from flying into windows in the US every year.
Pet cats are estimated to kill billions of birds in the US every year.
Most of the stars you can see at night will outlive everyone who has ever looked at them.
The only people known to have died in space were the three Soyuz 11 cosmonauts, in 1971.
Pluto was demoted to a dwarf planet in 2006, before it had finished even one trip around the Sun since its discovery in 1930.
The bristlecone pine called Prometheus, over 4,800 years old, was cut down in 1964 by a researcher studying it.

# Dark

Heroin was once sold by Bayer as a cough medicine.
Early Coca-Cola contained cocaine.
Some Victorian "soothing syrups" for teething babies contained morphine.
Bloodletting was a standard medical treatment for more than 2,000 years.
George Washington's doctors drained a large amount of his blood while treating him in the hours before he died.
Most of the plastic ever made still exists in some form.
Researchers have found microplastics in human blood.
The Great Pacific Garbage Patch covers an area about twice the size of Texas.
Mosquitoes are the deadliest animals to humans, because of the diseases they spread.
Sharks kill fewer than ten people a year on average, while people kill tens of millions of sharks.
Cows kill more people in the US each year than sharks do.
Bones are usually the last part of a body to remain.
Victorians often wore mourning jewelry woven from a dead relative's hair.
The Catacombs of Paris hold the remains of around six million people.
A crypt in Rome is decorated with the bones of about 3,700 friars.
Plague doctors stuffed the beaks of their masks with herbs, believing it would protect them from "bad air".
Ancient Romans believed drinking gladiator blood could cure epilepsy.
Doctors once tasted urine to help diagnose diabetes, because it tastes sweet.
A honeybee dies after stinging a mammal, because its barbed stinger tears out of its body.
Ötzi the Iceman died about 5,300 years ago with an arrowhead lodged in his shoulder.
The plaster figures at Pompeii were made by filling the hollows that bodies left in the volcanic ash.
Every atom in your body will still exist long after you're gone.
One leading theory says the universe will end in "heat death", when nothing can ever happen again.
Mars's moon Phobos is spiraling inward and will crash into Mars or break apart in about 50 million years.
Some deep-sea anglerfish males fuse permanently to a female and slowly lose most of their organs.
Medieval European cities often dumped waste straight into the street, sometimes from upstairs windows.
In some Victorian factories, matches were made with white phosphorus that destroyed workers' jawbones.
"Mad as a hatter" probably comes from the mercury poisoning that affected hat makers.
Before refrigeration, spoiled food was one of the most common causes of illness.
The guillotine was used for executions in France until 1977.
For most of human history, around half of all children died before reaching adulthood.
Some ancient Egyptian mummies were made of animals, and millions of mummified birds have been found.
Tapeworms can grow several meters long inside the human gut.
The body of the Russian revolutionary Vladimir Lenin has been preserved and on display in Moscow since 1924.
